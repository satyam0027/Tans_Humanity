Why Voice of Peace?

Violence May Divide. Humanity Can Unite.

Terrorism and violent extremism leave consequences that extend far beyond the moment of an attack. Individuals, families, communities and those who protect them can carry the impact for years.

Voice of Peace exists to listen, remember, connect and help build pathways towards peace.

We seek to create spaces where affected voices can be heard, communities can come together, young people can participate, and people from different backgrounds can stand together for peace.

A Beginning in Australia

Voice of Peace began its journey in Australia in 2016, with a commitment to better understand the human impact of terrorism and bring greater attention to the experiences of people affected by violence.

Over the years, that journey has connected us with affected families, police and military families, serving and former personnel, community members and citizens whose lives have been touched directly or indirectly by terrorism and related violence.

Their stories of courage, sacrifice, grief, hope and resilience helped shape the vision for Voice of Peace.

Now, a New Chapter Begins.

India marks the next chapter in the Voice of Peace journey.

From India, we seek to build connections across borders and bring people, organisations and communities together around a shared commitment to:

Peace. Unity. Compassion. Respect for Human Life.

The longer-term vision is to grow Voice of Peace into an international platform connecting communities and initiatives across countries and regions while remaining grounded in our shared humanity.

What We Do

Turning the Voice of Peace into Action

More Than a Rally. More Than a Concert. More Than a Campaign.

Voice of Peace is envisioned as an enduring international peace platform—bringing people together not only for moments of solidarity, but also through meaningful initiatives that raise awareness, strengthen communities and contribute to lasting peace.

Our initiatives will focus on:

Affected Voices & Families

Creating respectful opportunities for individuals and families affected by terrorism and violence to be heard, acknowledged and included in the broader conversation about peace and resilience.

Public Events

Creating visible moments of unity, solidarity and collective commitment to peace.

Remembrance

Honouring those affected by terrorism and violence, remembering lives lost and changed, and recognising the courage and resilience of families and communities.

Youth Engagement

Giving young people a meaningful voice and encouraging them to become ambassadors for peace, understanding and positive change.

Education & Awareness

Building understanding of the human impact of terrorism and violence while encouraging informed conversations about peace, inclusion and social cohesion.

Dialogue

Creating opportunities for people across cultures, faiths, generations and communities to listen, learn and build mutual understanding.

Arts & Culture

Using music, storytelling, creativity and shared cultural experiences to connect people and communicate messages of peace and humanity.

Community Engagement

Strengthening relationships, inclusion, compassion and resilience within and between communities.

International Collaboration

Building connections among organisations, communities and partners across countries to share knowledge, strengthen cooperation and advance a shared commitment to peace.

India Programme

Five Regional Humanitarian Events

The regional programme consists of five small, dignified humanitarian events across India. The first event is fixed for 4 October 2026 in Punjab. Four further events will follow in Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and Delhi, with exact dates and venues confirmed after coordination, permissions and operational planning.

Standard Regional Event Format

  1. Welcome and introduction to Voice of Peace.
  2. Peace and remembrance message.
  3. Honouring and recognition of selected families.
  4. Presentation of immediate humanitarian assistance.
  5. Announcement of the six-month support commitment.
  6. Family story or short film, only with informed consent.
  7. Local cultural or musical performance.
  8. Volunteer and community participation.
  9. Promotion of the December Delhi “Honouring Our Heroes” march.
  10. Media and digital awareness activity.
  11. Closing message: peace, dignity, unity and continued support.

Each event is intended to be relatively small and personal. The objective is not to create a large entertainment spectacle, but to create a genuine relationship between the Foundation, families, local communities and supporters.

Our Commitment to Inclusion

Voice of Peace does not associate terrorism with any particular religion, ethnicity, nationality or community.

We recognise that terrorism and violence can affect people of every background, and that lasting peace requires communities to work across differences rather than deepen them.

Voice of Peace seeks to provide an inclusive and respectful platform where people from different backgrounds can come together around their shared humanity.

We are committed to peaceful dialogue, dignity, mutual respect and the responsible treatment of the experiences and stories entrusted to us.

Our Vision

A World That Refuses to Be Divided by Violence.

We envision a world where people across cultures, faiths, nationalities and communities stand together against violence and hatred.

  • Where affected voices are heard.
  • Where those whose lives have been changed by violence are treated with dignity and compassion.
  • Where communities support one another.
  • Where differences become opportunities for understanding rather than division.
  • Where young people become ambassadors for peace.
  • Where dialogue builds bridges.
  • And where compassion is stronger than hatred and division.

Privacy & Safeguarding

Voice of Peace is committed to the dignity, privacy and safety of everyone who engages with our work — especially survivors, affected families and young people.

We treat personal stories as a trust. We do not share identifying details without consent, and we do not collect more information than we need in order to respond with care.

Enquiries sent through this website are intended only for the Voice of Peace team. Please do not include sensitive personal information about others unless you have their permission.

If you have a concern about privacy or safeguarding, please contact us and we will treat it with the seriousness it deserves.
